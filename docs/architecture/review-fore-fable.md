## Top 10 by importance

1. **FORE-FABLE-01** The factory has two machines and one of them is invisible: ship each host's `events/traces/loop/usage` to a shared ref so any review, digest or breaker sees both.
2. **FORE-FABLE-05** Flaky-test quarantine with an owner-labelled ledger: the same `tests/slots/real` and `tests/loop/real` ids fail in 7-12 red suite logs each; at 69 releases/day the suite is the factory's slowest, least trusted part.
3. **FORE-FABLE-03** A factory-wide circuit breaker and version canary: N loops each stop after 2 failures, nothing stops them together on a bad release; a new tl version should be "proven" by K landed beads before `move_to` may pick it.
4. **FORE-FABLE-02** The production config dir accepts writes from tests and fixtures (8 `/r` runs are in it now): refuse a run for an unregistered repo, and make `tl doctor` name foreign runs.
5. **FORE-FABLE-09** One subscription, two machines, one local lease table: 35 % of devbox calls were `busy`/`no_lane` while the cap was raised by hand from 4 to 8. Lane capacity and the weekly window must be accounted across hosts, with pacing.
6. **FORE-FABLE-07** Queue fairness has no aging and no starvation guard: 253 open P3/P4 lifesim beads can never run while P2 keeps arriving; the rank is `(priority, epic rank, lowest id)` and nothing else.
7. **FORE-FABLE-10** Token budgets are off by default (`bead_token_cap` 0) and cost is always 0: the only real budget is the weekly window. Default caps per bead and per day, and tokens on the dashboard per bead.
8. **FORE-FABLE-14** Automatic grooming: 122 of 497 closes were hand sweeps, 199 lifesim beads are deferred, 155 parked. A groomer that defers stale beads, closes empty epics and proposes duplicates (the Jaccard code exists in `domain/report/reflect.py`).
9. **FORE-FABLE-06** Replay a run from its JSONL: the engine trail (`engine/<run>.jsonl`, v3 rows) is written but nothing reads it back as a decision replay; every RCA this week was a human grepping.
10. **FORE-FABLE-04** Retention is per family and unbounded in places: 209 MB of suite logs in five days, delegate history rotated at 1 MB with silent loss; one retention table with a size budget.

## What I think you are NOT thinking about

1. **You are reviewing a two-host factory from one host's files.** Every document in `docs/architecture/` and `docs/review/` cites `~/.config/task-loop` on the devbox; the Mac's loop logs, parks, usage history and delegate history are not in any of them. The incident list exists only in the owner's head and in commit subjects. Until 01 lands, every "learning loop" (reflect digest, breaker, canary, grooming) runs on half the data and the half it misses is the Trust Night half.
2. **The release rate is your hop rate.** 69 releases in one day, beads of 25-90 minutes: a bead sees two or three newer installs during its life, and `safe_point` (`reload.py:68`) takes each one. Z05/R2 freezes the bundle; nothing proposes that the next version prove itself before any host moves to it. A canary needs the shared feed too.
3. **The suite is now the slowest, least trusted component.** 377 red suite logs in five days, the same real tests failing a dozen times each, and FLAKES4/FLAKES5 fixed by hand. Each red costs a rerun and a human judgement; at the current test growth (+55 % in four days) the suite's wall time and its flake rate both rise. No bead names a quarantine ledger or a time ratchet.
4. **Fairness is absent by design, and grooming is manual because of it.** `(priority, epic rank, lowest id)` means 253 open P3/P4 lifesim beads are never run; they age into the 199 deferred and the 155 parked, and the owner closes them in sweeps of 72. Aging in the rank (07) is a one-line change that removes most of the grooming load (14).
5. **The subscription is global; every counter is local.** Leases, `max_parallel`, the `busy` fold and the weekly-window reading all live in one host's `state.json`; the Mac and the devbox both drain `OPENAI_SUBSCRIPTION`. The cap was raised by hand from 4 to 8 on one host. The next incident is "the week is spent on Wednesday" and no file on either machine will say why.
6. **The config dir trusts anyone who can import `taskloop`.** Eight fake runs from a closeguard fixture sit in the real `runs/` today. The dashboard, `tl report` and the digest count them. The only guard is pytest's conftest; a unittest run, a Claude one-off or a future `tl replay` with the wrong env writes straight into production truth.
7. **Nothing measures whether the factory learns.** The reflect digest files beads; no number says how many it filed, how many were fixed, how many causes recurred after the fix. The 9 `reflect`-labelled beads are all the evidence there is.
8. **Model monoculture.** 160 of 162 rows are one model on one lane; the ZAI lane is `out` with every call falling back. One vendor regression stalls both factories and the scorecard that would show it does not exist.

## Where I disagree with the existing documents
